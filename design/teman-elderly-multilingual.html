What to watch while you switch

  • Nothing breaks at 26 px. Buttons grow with the text and labels wrap to two lines instead of truncating. Truncation is the single most common way an accessible-looking app fails an older user — the words they needed are the ones cut off.
  • Malay runs about 20% longer than English, Tamil longer still. That's why no button has a fixed width and no label sits in a horizontal row with three others.
  • The heading font changes with the script. Vollkorn covers no Tamil and no Chinese, so those switch to Noto Serif Tamil and Noto Serif SC — the serif voice survives, the fallback isn't left to the operating system.
  • Line-height changes too. Latin 1.55, Tamil 1.75, Chinese 1.8. Tamil conjuncts stack above and below the baseline; CJK glyphs need breathing room. Same number for all three would look cramped in two of them.
  • The language button never says only "Language". It carries a globe and sits in a fixed place at the bottom of the home screen — reachable by thumb, findable by someone who cannot read the current language.
02

The language switch is a safety feature

If an 72-year-old opens the app and it is in the wrong language, they don't switch it — they close it and don't come back. Worse, in a live session, a person who cannot read the screen cannot reach the safety button. So this is not a settings-screen item.

Entry point 1

First screen, before anything

Before account, before permissions, before the value proposition. Four large cards, each in its own script, shown simultaneously. Nothing else on the screen.

Entry point 2

Bottom of the home screen

A permanent globe button in the same place every time. Not buried under "You → Settings → Preferences → Language" — that is three reading tasks for someone who can't read the words.

Entry point 3

Every onboarding and verification screen

The screens most likely to be reached in the wrong language, and the ones where abandoning means losing a verified volunteer the NGO already recruited.

No flags. Not one.

A flag names a country, not a language. Malaysian Tamil speakers are Malaysian — showing them an Indian flag is wrong and it lands badly. Same for a Chinese flag next to 中文, and a British flag next to English. Language is named in its own script, and only in its own script.

The picker is never translated

All four options always appear together in their own writing, in the same order, every time. A person looking for தமிழ் scans for the shape they recognise. If the list re-labels itself into the current language, the shape they're looking for disappears.

A Malaysian household is often multilingual across generations. The caregiver may set the app up in English and hand the phone to a parent who reads only Tamil. Language is a per-profile setting, not a per-device one.

Type specs per script

One size and one line-height across four scripts is the wrong answer in three of them. These are the numbers to implement.

Never build a sentence from fragments

"3" + " people need a Teman" breaks in every language with different word order. Every string is whole, with the number as a named placeholder.

Dates and times are localised too

"Friday, 9:30 AM" is not "Jumaat, 9:30 pagi" by string swap. Use the platform date formatter per locale, including the 12/24-hour convention.

Never machine-translate safety copy

SOS wording, consent text, the "not an emergency service" notice and the emotional-support disclaimer are translated by a human and reviewed by the NGO. These are the strings where a wrong word is a real-world harm.

Every row below is a correction to the v1 system, with the reason. The contrast numbers were computed against the rendered pixels, not estimated from the hex codes.

05

Interaction rules

Grouped by what actually changes with age — vision, motor control, and memory load. Each of these is checkable in review.

Vision
  • Body text 18 px minimum, user-scalable to 26 px.
  • Never a font weight below 400. No thin or light weights anywhere.
  • AAA (7:1) for all body text; 3:1 minimum for every border, icon and input outline.
  • No placeholder-as-label. Placeholders vanish on focus and older users lose the field's purpose.
  • Blue–yellow discrimination declines with age. Amber never carries meaning alone — it always appears with the Sisi shape and a word.
  • No pure white background. Neutral 50 reduces glare.
Hands
  • 64 px primary targets, 56 px absolute floor, 12 px clear between any two.
  • No gesture is ever the only route. Swipe-to-delete always has a visible button too.
  • No long-press, no double-tap, no drag.
  • Primary actions sit in the lower half of the screen, within thumb reach.
  • No session timeouts on any form. A person may put the phone down to find their reading glasses.
  • Destructive actions confirm on a full screen, never a small dialog with a tiny X.
Memory & attention
  • One decision per screen. The request flow is seven short screens, not one long form.
  • Controls stay in the same place on every screen. Consistency beats efficiency here.
  • Progress is always visible: "Step 3 of 7", never a bare spinner.
  • No hamburger menu. Four flat tabs, always labelled.
  • Nothing auto-advances, auto-plays, or auto-dismisses. A toast that disappears was never read.
  • Every screen can be left without losing what was entered.
06

Two doors into the same product

The scope has a family member creating requests on behalf of a parent, and it also has the parent using the app directly. These are different people with different capabilities, and one home screen cannot serve both. The account chooses its door at setup and can change it any time.

Caregiver view — the default

Three actions, the profiles they manage, a request list with states, and a calendar. This is the person who is comfortable with apps, on a commute, with limited time. Density is acceptable here.

Elder view — the one that matters

Strip to what a person alone at home actually needs. Two or three actions, no counters, no discovery, no browsing. Everything else — availability, going-there-too, community circles — is hidden, not shrunk.

  • Someone to come with me — appointment or errand
  • Someone to talk to — company, not therapy
  • Call my family — always visible, always the same place

Text size defaults to Large in this view, not Standard.

07

Review checklist

Run this on every screen before it ships. Any single failure blocks it — these are the things that decide whether a 72-year-old finishes the flow or puts the phone down.

Per screen
  • Readable at 26 px text size with nothing truncated or overlapping
  • Correct in all four languages, including the longest string
  • Every control has a visible word, not just an icon
  • Every border and input outline reaches 3:1
  • All body text reaches 7:1
  • Exactly one primary action, in the lower half of the screen
  • Empty, loading and error states exist and are written in the brand voice
  • Keyboard focus is visible and follows a sensible order
  • Nothing is communicated by colour alone
Before the NGO pilot
  • Tested on a low-end Android phone, not just a recent iPhone
  • Tested at the operating system's own largest font setting, on top of the in-app size
  • Screen reader passes in all four languages with correct lang attributes
  • Safety and consent copy translated by a human and signed off by the NGO
  • Watched — silently — while five people over 65 complete one request each
  • Every place they hesitated is written down. That list is the next sprint.

No one should have to go alone.

Which means the app must not be the thing they face alone either.
