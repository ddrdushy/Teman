Why this exists

Loneliness in a city is rarely a logistics problem.

Most people can get themselves to the hospital. What they can't do is walk in with nobody waiting for them to come out. Meanwhile the same neighbourhood is full of people with two free hours and no obvious way to give them away.

Safety

Strangers meeting in real life. We treat that seriously.

Not with a promise — with specifics you can check.

1

A person checks every ID

MyKad or passport, matched against a selfie by a human reviewer at a partner organisation. Not an algorithm.

90

Days, then the document is gone

Deleted automatically after review. We never store the ID number itself — only a scrambled version used to spot duplicates.

2

Both people must agree

Until you both accept, only the general area is visible. The exact address and phone number unlock at that moment, and we record every time they're seen.

0

Ratings out of five

Trust is shown as verification, the number of times someone has shown up, and words other members used. A score would turn a neighbour into a gig worker.

Teman is not an emergency service. If someone is in danger or needs medical help right now, call 999 first. Our safety button sits alongside that, never instead of it.

Talk to us

Questions

The ones people actually ask.

Is it really free? What's the catch?+

Yes, and there isn't one. No payments between members, no subscription, no commission, no tipping, no ads. Teman is run with an NGO partner and costs you nothing at any point. If anyone on the platform asks you for money, report it — that's the single most common way people get taken advantage of, and it's why we say it this bluntly.

Who are these people, really?+

Neighbours — mostly people with a few free hours who wanted a way to use them. Every Teman gives a phone number and a photo, and most complete a MyKad check reviewed by a person at a partner organisation. You see their record, their languages, how many times they've shown up and what other members said about them, before you decide anything.

What if nobody comes?+

Sometimes nobody can. If your request hasn't found anyone, we tell you the evening before — not on the morning — and suggest what else you could try, like a different time or a community centre nearby. We'd rather say it plainly than let you find out when you're already at the door.

What happens if something goes wrong?+

There's a safety button in every session: contact your trusted person, call 999, share your location, or report what's happening. A serious report stops that person from being matched with anyone immediately, before a human has even reviewed it. Reports are read by a person, usually within a day, and we tell you what was decided.

My father doesn't have a smartphone.+

That's the common case, and the app is built for it. You create a profile for him, you make the requests, and you get every update — when they meet, when the session starts, when it ends. He never needs to touch a phone. You can also set his preferred language separately from yours, so if he's most comfortable in Tamil we look for a Tamil speaker first.

Can I ask for a woman for my mother?+

Yes. You can ask for women only, men only, or no preference, alongside language, age range and whether the person needs to drive. Fewer preferences usually means finding someone sooner, but nobody is ever matched against a preference you set.

Is this a dating app?+

No, and romantic or sexual approaches are against the guidelines in messages and in person. It's one of the report categories, and it's treated seriously.
